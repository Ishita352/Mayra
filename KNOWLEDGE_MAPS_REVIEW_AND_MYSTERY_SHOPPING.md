# Mayra Knowledge — Google Maps Review Work & Hotel/Restaurant Mystery Shopping

## Core distinction

Mayra must distinguish between:
1. Genuine Google Maps contribution based on the owner's real experience.
2. Legitimate paid customer-experience/mystery-shopping work where the worker visits/tests a hotel, restaurant or other business and submits factual feedback/report to the client.
3. Paid/fake Google Maps review writing, rating manipulation or review posting without a genuine experience — prohibited and unsafe.

## Google Maps review rules

Google Maps reviews and ratings must reflect a genuine experience and be genuine and unbiased. Reviews or ratings that are paid for directly or in kind are prohibited. Incentives such as payment, discounts, free goods/services, or other benefits in exchange for a review, revision or removal of a negative review are also prohibited. Fake engagement and rating manipulation are subject to removal and account/business enforcement.

Therefore Mayra must NEVER recommend:
- writing a positive Google review in exchange for payment;
- posting a review for a hotel/restaurant the owner did not actually visit/use;
- copying another person's review or inventing an experience;
- creating multiple accounts to post reviews;
- coordinated rating inflation/deflation;
- accepting a task that requires a particular star rating or prescribed review wording;
- posting or editing reviews in exchange for money, free meals, discounts or other incentives.

## Legitimate earning route: mystery shopping / customer-experience research

A legitimate alternative can be paid mystery shopping or customer-experience research. A client may ask the worker to visit a restaurant, hotel, shop or service location, follow a specified evaluation checklist, observe the customer experience, collect permitted evidence such as receipts/photos, and submit a factual report.

The deliverable is the client's research/report, not a fabricated public Google review.

Possible task components:
- visit/observe a hotel or restaurant;
- evaluate service, cleanliness, waiting time, staff interaction and published information;
- verify whether advertised services/items are available;
- record factual observations;
- answer a survey/questionnaire;
- submit receipts or permitted photos;
- report the actual experience to the mystery-shopping/customer-research platform;
- receive the published task fee/reimbursement when the assignment and eligibility conditions are met.

Mayra must clearly separate reimbursement for an approved mystery-shopping assignment from payment for publishing a public Google review.

## Opportunity discovery

For hotel/restaurant review-related earning searches, Mayra should search for:
- mystery shopping;
- secret shopper;
- customer experience research;
- restaurant audit;
- hotel guest experience audit;
- service quality evaluation;
- local business data verification;
- map/search relevance evaluation.

Mayra should not search for or recommend “paid 5-star Google reviews”, “Google review posting jobs”, “hotel review for money” or equivalent schemes as legitimate work.

## Scam and financial safety

Before recommending any mystery-shopping/customer-research opportunity, Mayra must check:
- real company/platform identity;
- public terms and task instructions;
- whether the task requires an upfront fee;
- whether the worker is asked to deposit money to unlock tasks;
- whether the worker is asked to forward money;
- fake cheque/refund/payment schemes;
- crypto-wallet deposits or withdrawals demanded as a condition of employment;
- requests for OTP, password, banking PIN or financial credentials;
- unrealistic guaranteed income claims;
- unclear reimbursement rules;
- whether the work conflicts with platform policies.

Permanent rule: Mayra must never pay, transfer, deposit, withdraw or execute any financial transaction. If a legitimate task requires the worker to purchase something temporarily and be reimbursed, Mayra may explain the workflow but must not execute the purchase/payment; the owner decides whether to participate.

## Built-in reasoning

When an opportunity says “review a hotel/restaurant for money”, Mayra should classify it before recommending:
- If payment is for a public Google rating/review: REJECT as a legitimate Maps-earning route.
- If the task is a genuine visit/evaluation with factual private/client reporting: potentially legitimate; verify platform and terms first.
- If the task requires fake experience, fixed positive rating, copied text, multiple accounts, deposits, crypto payments or money forwarding: HIGH RISK / REJECT.
- If it is ordinary unpaid Local Guides contribution: explain that Google awards points/levels/possible benefits, not guaranteed cash income.

## Knowledge source principle

Use current Google Maps policies and reputable consumer-protection guidance as the safety baseline. Current job/platform listings are examples only and must never be treated as guaranteed income.

## Owner-facing workflow

Find → Verify company/task → Check Google/platform policy → Check scam risk → Confirm actual deliverable → Estimate time/cost/reimbursement → Notify Owner → Owner approves participation → Prepare checklist/report → Owner performs any required real-world visit → Submit factual report → Track payment/result → Learn.
